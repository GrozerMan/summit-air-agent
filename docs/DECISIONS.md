# Decisions and trade-offs

## 1. LiveKit Agents, not Vapi/Retell, and not a hand-built pipeline
**Chose:** LiveKit Agents (open source, Python) on LiveKit Cloud, with a LiveKit phone number and LiveKit Inference for STT/LLM/TTS.
**Why:** the agent lives in our repo, so every behaviour is a code change we can test and debug live, and tools are in-process function calls rather than webhooks to a server we'd also have to host. One vendor account covers the phone number, models, turn detection and noise cancellation, which keeps a demo's moving parts to one. LiveKit ships a text-mode test harness, which made a real eval suite cheap.
**Gave up:** Vapi/Retell get to a first live call faster and include a no-code dashboard. A hand-built Twilio Media Streams pipeline gives total control but would spend the week on barge-in and endpointing instead of Summit Air's problems.
**Twilio:** not required. LiveKit sells numbers directly; an existing Twilio number can be pointed at LiveKit via SIP trunk with no code change.

## 2. The LLM never decides urgency, eligibility, availability or price
The model extracts facts (`gas_smell`, `no_heat`, `indoor_temp_f`, `vulnerable_occupant`...) and `summit/triage.py` applies ordered rules. Same facts, same answer, every time; each call records which rules fired, so the owner can read *why* a call was P1. Thresholds are config.
The same pattern holds for booking: the backend refuses rather than trusting the prompt. Prompt rules are for tone; code rules are for anything that costs money or safety.

## 3. Safety is conservative and short
Gas, CO and smoke/fire are P0 regardless of anything else, including "the smell went away": once a hazard is reported on a call, a later "no" doesn't clear it. The script is fixed text (get out, don't touch switches or phones inside, call nine-one-one or the county's gas utility from outside), taken from National Grid and Con Edison's own guidance. When we don't know the county yet, the script says "nine-one-one or your gas company" rather than stopping an evacuation to ask which town they're in; a town or ZIP said in the same sentence as the hazard is used, and the dispatcher alert carries the caller's exact words. A booking made earlier on the same call goes on hold, so a technician doesn't arrive at a reported leak with a routine ticket. The agent doesn't collect booking details first, and lets a caller who is still inside hang up. Priority never goes down within a call, so a calmer second description can't undo a gas report.

## 4. Identity before account details
Caller ID alone is not proof (shared phones, spouses, spoofing). The greeting asks "Am I speaking with Dana?"; membership, address and history are only disclosed after that, and no lookup runs until the model reports the answer (a son who never says "no" used to verify as his mother with her ZIP). From any other number it takes the account's phone, last name *and* ZIP: a phone number and a surname are easy for a stranger to know. A "no" changes the rules for the rest of the call: caller ID stops counting as a factor, their own account needs its phone, last name *and* ZIP, and the account they said they aren't stays closed even if they know all three (the stress test's "No, I'm her son... last name Reyes, ZIP 11501" used to verify him as his mother). Three lookups per call, so a ZIP can't be guessed. A household number shared by several customers isn't greeted by name at all. Unverified callers get non-member pricing, and an unverified booking gets its own customer record (provisional, out of caller ID and lookups, when its number matches an account), with a "possible match" note for the office. It used to attach to the account holder, who then saw, and could move, a stranger's visit. Memberships past their expiry date count as lapsed. An unknown caller is asked once, before times are offered, whether they're already a member, so a member calling from a new number isn't charged or kept out of member capacity.

## 5. Arrival windows and capacity pools
Real HVAC dispatch books 2-hour arrival windows, not appointments. Capacity has three pools: regular, a daily emergency reserve only P1 calls can use (so an urgent call never hears "next Thursday"), and a member-priority pool (a concrete benefit to sell). Routine work never takes same-day capacity. Pre-existing load is simulated deterministically so demos and tests are repeatable.

## 6. Read-backs are checked, not taken on trust
Phone number and address are the two fields that waste a truck roll when wrong. `book_appointment` requires `phone_read_back_confirmed` and `address_read_back_confirmed`, and those flags alone used to be the model's word: told "don't read anything back, just book it", it set both to true and booked. Now the backend also checks the agent's own transcript for the ten digits (in any spoken form) and the house number and street. What it can't check is the caller's "yes"; that is still the model's report. It also rejects a ZIP that differs from the one checked against the service area. When something's missing it lists everything at once, so the agent asks once rather than three times.

## 7. Honest fallbacks over pretend capability
No live transfer line? The agent says so and creates a callback with a due time. No urgent capacity? Dispatcher alert plus a 30-minute callback, said plainly. Tool results tell the model exactly what to say. A booking confirmation is spoken as fixed text from the booking record, and at hang-up any claim of a booking or callback the records don't show raises an urgent alert and a callback. The model can still word a transfer request badly ("I'll connect you" with no line configured); that one is prompt-only.

## 8. Commercial callers become leads, not rejections
Summit Air is residential-only, so no booking, but a commercial caller is still revenue the owner may want; the agent offers a callback rather than a dead end. Out-of-area callers get a kind no with a suggestion, and no callback unless asked.

## 9. Membership revenue, lightly
Active members are thanked and get their benefits, lapsed members get one gentle renewal mention after their problem is handled, and non-members hear about the club at most once, after booking.

## 10. Evals that grade outcomes, not wording
Scripted multi-turn tests break whenever the model asks things in a different order. The eval harness has an LLM play a caller persona against the real agent and grades primarily on backend state (was it booked, at what priority, was the dispatcher alerted, was the booking moved), with an LLM judge only for tone. Backend state alone passed an agent that told a gas caller to stay home, because the backend was right and only the words were wrong. So the grader also checks the words that matter: the P0 script verbatim with nothing reassuring alongside it, the protected account's own values never spoken to an unverified caller, and no claimed booking or callback without a record. `evals/stress/grader_audit.py` keeps the grader honest with wrong agents that must fail and right ones that must pass. Eval calls run at a fixed time on a fixed calendar, so a failure is the model's doing, not the clock's.

## 11. One connection per call, a write lock for bookings; SQLite locally, Postgres deployed
Each call has its own connection; the seat check and the insert happen inside one immediate transaction, so two callers racing for the last seat can't both get it. `test_the_lock_is_what_prevents_overbooking` takes the lock away and shows the race overbooking; `test_with_the_lock_a_four_way_race_has_one_winner` puts it back. Moving an appointment checks it is still booked inside the same lock, so two calls moving the same appointment can't both succeed. On Postgres (the deployed agent) the same lock is a transaction-scoped advisory lock; `test_on_postgres_a_four_way_race_has_one_winner` runs against a real Postgres, and without the lock that race overbooks 4 of 4. The SQL is written once in SQLite's dialect and translated, so the tests run on either (`SUMMIT_TEST_POSTGRES=1`).

## 12. The model is not the only way into P0
**Found by test call P0-01.** The caller said the furnace smelled "kinda like a skunk... earlier today, but now it's gone". The model called `assess_situation` with `gas_smell=false` (the description it passed even said "skunk-like"), triage scored P3, and the agent offered three appointment windows. It escalated only after the caller asked "do you not care that it might be a gas leak? It smells like rotten eggs." Decision 2 held: the LLM didn't decide urgency. But the LLM did decide the *input* to the rule, so the guarantee was only as good as one non-deterministic extraction.
**Chose:** a deterministic hazard lexicon (`summit/hazard_words.py`: rotten eggs, sulfur, skunk, smell-words near gas/propane, a sounding CO alarm or CO symptoms, smoke, sparks and flames) at three layers, any of which reaches P0 on its own:
1. **Before the model:** `SummitAgent.llm_node` screens each caller turn. On a hit, the backend goes to P0 (alert, outcome, offered windows voided) and the safety script is spoken as fixed text without an LLM call. `llm_node` rather than an STT event so it works for phone calls, `console --text` and the evals alike.
2. **In triage:** `assess()` runs the lexicon on the model's `description`, so `gas_smell=false` with "skunk-like" in the description is still P0.
3. **In the backend:** finding, booking, moving and rescheduling all refuse on P0. Refusal is checked in one place.
**Also:** spoken scripts say "nine-one-one" and spell the utility number group by group (the model had said "nine hundred and eleven"). "I can't / don't feel safe leaving" after the script gets a fixed answer rather than an improvised one, and for the rest of a P0 call every model turn carries a standing system note. That note first told the model to bring every answer back to leaving, and it repeated the script to callers who had already said goodbye; it now says the script has been read, not to repeat it, and to say one goodbye and end the call once the caller is leaving. Alerts for unverified callers say "caller ID matches X" rather than naming X as the caller.
**Gave up:** some false positives, on purpose. A P0 raised by the word list is as permanent as any other: undoing an emergency is the riskier mistake, so there is no "are you sure?" question that could talk a caller out of it. Instead the wording that caused false alarms is fixed (a question that got a no, "I asked my wife if it smells like gas and she said no"; a skunk living under the porch with no smell mentioned), and if a word-list-only P0 still turns out wrong, the agent offers a callback from the office for the visit. A denial only counts when the negating word sits right before the hazard ("I don't smell gas", "no smoke or sparks"): "it won't stop smelling like gas" and "I can't stand the rotten egg smell" are not denials, and a denial of now ("not anymore", "not now") is an admission of earlier, which stays P0. Hedges ("not sure, but it smells like gas") still escalate. Fire words carry HVAC carve-outs ("won't fire", "fire up", a spark igniter, a chirping smoke detector). A dusty first-use smell, or "gas furnace", doesn't fire; those are pinned as negative-control tests.

## 13. Serve NYC, and never turn a customer away on a town name
The brief says a residential HVAC company in NYC across three counties. The first build read that as Nassau, Suffolk and Westchester with a curated 32-town list, which told callers in Syosset, Merrick and Elmont they were out of area and turned away every NYC caller. Now the five boroughs are served too, every ZIP in the served counties comes from GeoNames, and town names are normalized ("Rockville Center") and fuzzy-matched with a confirmation. A town the agent doesn't know, or one in two counties, gets "what's the ZIP?", never a no; only a ZIP or another state is a definite no. Which counties are served is one line of config, the first thing to confirm with the owner.
**NYC specifics:** a renter whose heat comes from the building's boiler is the landlord's responsibility (and 311's, under the city's heat law), so the agent says so and offers a callback rather than booking; a building owner of five or more units is a commercial lead. Queens' gas service is split between utilities, so a Queens gas caller hears "nine-one-one or your gas company" rather than a number that may be wrong.

## 14. No heat in winter is urgent on its own
The owner listed "no heat in winter" as urgent. Triage had made it P2 unless it was 58°F or below inside or someone vulnerable was home. Now, from Oct 1 to May 31, no heat is P1 unless the caller says it's at least 65°F inside. The cost: in a cold snap every no-heat call can draw on the emergency reserve, which then runs out sooner, and later callers get an urgent dispatcher callback instead of a window. That's the right failure for this owner, and the reserve size is config.

## 15. The hazard screen, second pass
A round-2 probe suite tried to get around each fix. The worst find: the agent asks "any gas smell right now?" and "No, I don't smell gas right now" made the call a permanent P0, because "now" was read as admitting the past. A bare "right now" is now a plain no; "anymore" or "but it did earlier" still count. Other false alarms are fixed with narrow carve-outs, each pinned by tests on both sides: "it's smoking hot in here" (a heat-wave caller got the evacuation script), a husband who smokes, a fire in the fireplace, igniter sparks, blue burner flames, Fire Island, "giving me a headache". One stays on purpose: a household sick with "the flu" while the heating misbehaves is still CO, because that is how CO poisoning usually presents.

## Cut for time (and what I'd do next)
- **System of record:** real ServiceTitan / Housecall Pro integration behind `Store` (customer, location, job type, capacity, booking, notes).
- **SMS:** booking confirmation and "tech is on the way" texts; SMS as a second inbound channel.
- **Weather-aware triage:** outdoor temperature from a forecast API to sharpen P1 (no heat at 15°F outside vs 55°F).
- **Spanish:** STT/TTS and the turn detector support it; needs prompt and safety-script translation reviewed by a native speaker.
- **Paging on-call:** after-hours P1 alerts reach the dashboard and Slack; production wants SMS or a phone page with acknowledgement.
- **Call recording + QA sampling:** LiveKit session reports and recordings exist; wire a weekly review of P0/P1 calls.
- **Real address validation:** a geocoder to confirm the street exists and settle ZIPs that straddle counties.
